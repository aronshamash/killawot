Have you ever seen a camel run? Camel racing is one of the fastest and most exciting sports in the world. A racing camel can run faster than a car drives through a town. In this guide, you will learn what camel racing is, how fast camels can go, and why some racing camels carry tiny robots on their backs instead of people.

## What Is Camel Racing?

Camel racing is a sport where camels run as fast as they can along a long, sandy track. It is a bit like a horse race, but with camels instead of horses. People have raced camels for thousands of years in the deserts of the Middle East, where camels have always been important animals for desert life.

Today, camel racing is still popular in places like the United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and Australia. A big camel race can have hundreds of camels running at the same time.

## How Fast Can a Racing Camel Run?

Racing camels are surprisingly quick. A racing camel can sprint at up to 40 miles per hour (about 65 kilometres per hour). That is faster than most cars drive on a neighbourhood street. Over longer distances, a racing camel can keep running at around 25 miles per hour (40 kilometres per hour) for an hour or more.

Camels are built for the desert. Their long legs and wide, padded feet help them run fast on soft sand without sinking in. It works a bit like snowshoes help people walk on snow.

## Why Do Camels Have Robot Jockeys?

Here is one of the strangest and coolest facts about modern camel racing: many racing camels are ridden by robots, not people.

A jockey is the rider who sits on top of a racing animal. In the past, camel jockeys were often children. That was dangerous, so it is not allowed anymore. To keep the sport safe, camel racing now uses small robot jockeys instead.

A robot jockey is a lightweight machine strapped to the camel's back. A trainer drives alongside the track and controls the robot with a remote control. The remote can even give the camel a gentle tap so it knows to run faster. This keeps children safe, and the camels still get to race at full speed.

## Where Does Camel Racing Happen?

Camel racing happens all over the world, but it is most famous in desert countries. Here are a few places where you can find real camel races:

- **United Arab Emirates** — home to some of the biggest camel racing tracks in the world, with races held almost every week during the season
- **Saudi Arabia** — hosts the King Abdulaziz Camel Festival, one of the largest camel events on the planet
- **Qatar** — camel racing is a national sport here, with its own custom-built racetracks
- **Australia** — the Alice Springs Camel Cup is a fun outback race for professional and amateur riders

Here are some quick facts to remember about camel racing:

1. A racing camel can run faster than a car on a city street.
2. Robot jockeys have replaced child riders to keep the sport safe.
3. Camel races can be several kilometres long — much longer than a horse race.
4. A champion racing camel can be worth as much as a luxury car.
5. Camel racing has been part of Middle Eastern culture for thousands of years.

## Why People Love Camel Racing

Camel racing is exciting because it mixes speed, tradition, and clever technology. Watching a line of camels sprint across the desert, guided by tiny robot jockeys, is something you will not see anywhere else in the world of sport.

If you ever get the chance to watch a camel race in person or online, keep an eye on how fast those long legs move. It is faster than most people expect from such a big animal.
